## FS-1 — The glucose feed is 500 g/L (~42 wt%), not "50 wt%", and the "65 wt%" sensitivity is ordinary industrial practice. The headline titer ceiling is an artefact of this.
**major · error (mislabelled unit) with a consequence for a headline result**
Where: report Summary result 1 (p.1), §2.3 "Why a ceiling exists" (p.5-6), §4.4 and Table 7 (p.13-14), §5.5 and §6 (p.19); `data/pf/base.yaml:91`; `src/pftea/pf_ferm.py:408`; `scripts/run_analyses.py:197-201`; `docs/REVIEW.md` A4.

Comment: the model's `feed_glucose_wt` is a mass fraction in a balance that carries liquid at 1 kg/L (`dM = glc_kg_h / w_s + ...`, RHO = 1000), so `0.5` delivers 2 L of solution per kg of glucose — i.e. a **500 g/L** feed, exactly Intelligen's "500 g/L glucose solution", as `base.yaml` itself says. A true 50 wt% dextrose solution is ~1.23 kg/L and carries ~615 g/L; so the report's "50 wt% glucose feed" is really ~42 wt%, and the relaxation it presents as ambitious, "a 65% feed", is 650 g/L ≈ 49 true wt% — i.e. *less* concentrated than what routine processes already use. Your own evidence base contains the counter-examples: Shemesh & Fishman 2024 (the source of your 93-169 g/L cell-density range) feed "a 60% (w/v) sterile solution" of dextrose in a 30 °C, pH 5 K. phaffii fed-batch; Aro 2023 feeds "55 % glucose"; Nielsen et al. ## FS-2 — 4 ATP per peptide bond is a polymerisation-only bound; your own thermodynamics gives a tighter and better-founded ceiling (~0.50 g/g), and the physical-limit yield of 0.55 g/g sits above it
**major · unsupported/overclaimed**
Where: report §2.2 "Energetic ceiling" (p.4-5); `data/pf/base.yaml:65-69`; `src/pftea/product.py:113-118`; `src/pftea/plant.py:130-135`; `data/pf/scenarios/physical_limit.yaml:12`.

Comment: 4 ATP/peptide bond (Martin 2025) is the *ribosomal* cost and nothing else. It excludes the net ATP of amino-acid synthesis from glucose and NH₃ (~1-2 ATP-equivalents per residue on Stouthamer-type accounting), assimilatory sulfate reduction (~2-4 ATP + 4 NADPH per S; BLG carries 9 S per 162 residues ≈ 0.9 ATP-equivalents per residue, which alone is ~3-4% of the ceiling, more than the 1.3-1.6% booked in audit R1-F11), ER translocation and BiP/PDI folding cycles, disulfide formation via Ero1 (an extra O₂ cost, not in the balance), and — the big one in practice — ERAD/turnover of the misfolded fraction. Calling the result "an upper bound" is fair, but the report then uses it twice as if it were quantitative: "the base yield is 80% of it", and the physical-limit yield of 0.55 g/g as "~0.95 × the energetic ceiling".

What I checked (`ceiling2.py`, `ceiling3.py`): with 21.76 ATP/glucose the ceiling is 0.582 (BLG) / 0.590 (OVA) at 4 ATP/residue, 0.547 at 5.5, 0.537 at 6, 0.497 at 8. Inverting: Y_ps = 0.465 corresponds to an all-in budget of **9.9 ATP/residue**, and Y_ps = 0.55 to only **5.4 ATP/residue** — i.e. 1.4 ATP/residue for amino-acid synthesis, sulfate reduction, folding, secretion and turnover combined. Independently, running the model's own Heijnen-Tijhuis machinery on the *product* as if it were biomass (`th.GrowthModel(protein, glucose, 30 °C)`, the same correlation that reproduces the measured maintenance-corrected biomass yields 0.544-0.584) gives **0.501 g/g for BLG and 0.508 for OVA** — equivalent to ~7.9 ATP/residue. On that basis the base Y_ps is 93% of a defensible bound (not 80%), and the physical-limit 0.55 is 10% *above* it, which would require secreted-protein synthesis to dissipate less Gibbs energy than growth.

Fix: report both bounds side by side (ATP-based 0.58, Heijnen-based 0.50), take the lower as the working ceiling, cap the physical-limit yield at ≤0.50, and requote "80% of the ceiling" as "~93%". Effect on the floor, measured at the published optimum (X_prod 80, t_prod 72, 400 m³): Y_ps 0.55 → $6.107 (reproduces the report); 0.50 → **$6.47 (+5.9%)**; 0.465 → $6.81 (+11%).

## Answers to `docs/REVIEW.md` section A
1. **Production-phase glucose use.** Closer to the low end than to Intelligen's 0.28: I would expect 0.08-0.19 g/g effective (≈ qs 0.02-0.05 g/g/h at 107 g/L cells), not 0.276. Confidence ~75%. See FS-4.
2. **Highest sustained q_p for a secreted food protein.** ~5-20 mg/g/h in the best fungal enzyme processes (Ellilä 2017 Table 1: 0.20-0.42 g/L/h at 20-50 g DCW/L), and ~1-5 mg/g/h in K. phaffii. I have never seen 40 mg/g/h sustained for a heterologous secreted protein, and I would treat >20 mg/g/h as unevidenced. Confidence ~80% on the 5-20 band for fungi; ~85% that 40 mg/g/h is unattained today. See FS-3.
3. **Cell density.** 107 g/L at the start of production and 150 g/L maximum are realistic for K. phaffii on glucose (Shemesh 2024: 93-169 g/L on dextrose; >100 g/L DCW is the usual "ultra-high density" definition). The practical limit at scale is O₂ transfer, heat and viscosity rather than biology, and all three are modelled. Confidence ~85%.
4. **Minimum start and feed strength.** 30% of vessel is a defensible minimum (impeller submergence; Intelligen 30.5%, Impossible's patent 32%); no complaint. The feed concentration is the problem: 500 g/L is the bottom of industrial practice, and 600-700 g/L is routine (Shemesh 2024's 60% w/v dextrose; Novonesis's DoE at 52 w/w%; syrup delivered at 70-75% DS and heat-traced). Confidence ~85% that ≥600 g/L is standard at scale. See FS-1.
5. **OVA solubility.** I know of no measured limit at pH 5-6 in fermentation broth, and I would not expect solubility to bind below ~100 g/L. The constraint that will actually bind for T. reesei OVA is proteolysis (Aro's 36 kDa clip), not solubility. Confidence ~70% on solubility; ~80% on proteolysis being the real cap. See FS-8.
6. **Titers.** Yes — 13.88 g/L (Yao 2026, ethanol-induced) and 5.14 g/L on glucose (Li 2026) are, to my knowledge, the highest peer-reviewed BLG titers, and 2 g/L (Aro 2023) the highest peer-reviewed bioreactor OVA. Confidence ~80%. Two rows deserve a decision before publication: `titers.csv` row 15 (Lv 2025's secondary "P_AOX1 lacto-protein 5.4 g/L", primary Lv et al. Green Chem 25:5460 (2023) not on disk — if that protein is BLG it beats Li 2026 on glucose), and row 27 (Yang 2009, 5.45 g/L *quail* OVA in shake flask, abstract only, ~1,000× the next-best yeast OVA) — correctly excluded, but it is the one claim that would change the OVA picture if it replicated.
